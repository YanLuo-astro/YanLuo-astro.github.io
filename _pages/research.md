My research aims to improve the empirical foundations of **pre-main-sequence (PMS) stellar evolution**. Ages and masses of young stars are fundamental to studies of star and planet formation, but they remain strongly model-dependent. I use spectroscopy, photometry, astrometry, and large survey datasets to identify and calibrate the physical effects that bias standard evolutionary inferences.

## Starspots and young-star parameters

Young low-mass stars often have highly inhomogeneous photospheres. Cool starspots alter broadband colors, spectral types, effective temperatures, luminosities, and absorption-line strengths, so they can bias ages and masses inferred from the HR diagram.

My current work combines multi-epoch spectroscopy and photometric monitoring to study the connection between **starspots and the observed lithium dispersion** in young stars. I am also developing two-temperature spectral models to quantify spot temperature contrasts and covering fractions and to propagate these effects into stellar parameters and evolutionary ages.

## Lithium as an age diagnostic

Lithium depletion provides an important, independent constraint on PMS evolution. However, observed Li I 6708 Å equivalent widths can also respond to surface temperature structure and stellar activity. I am interested in separating true abundance evolution from photospheric effects and in using lithium together with HR-diagram positions, rotation, and activity to test young-star ages.

## Gaia-era tests of PMS evolution

**Gaia** makes it possible to test evolutionary models across large, homogeneous samples of young stars. I am interested in using Gaia spectroscopy and astrometry to study coeval populations, especially:

- the apparent age spread and mass-dependent age trends in young associations;
- wide binaries as coeval benchmarks for evolutionary tracks;
- astrometric binaries and dynamical masses as direct tests of model-predicted masses;
- empirical corrections for surface inhomogeneities in HR diagrams and color-magnitude diagrams.

## Optical-to-infrared spectroscopy of cool stars

I am also exploring the combination of **Gaia XP** and **SPHEREx** spectroscopy to construct empirical optical-to-infrared spectral sequences. These data provide a new route to studying temperature-dependent spectral morphology, stellar surface inhomogeneities, and the infrared properties of young stars on a very large scale.

## Broader goal

The long-term goal is to connect several independent observables—luminosity, effective temperature, lithium, rotation, surface inhomogeneities, and dynamical mass—into a more reliable empirical calibration of PMS stellar evolution and age determination.
